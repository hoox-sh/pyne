\begin{abstract}
Independent reimplementations of financial domain-specific languages (\textsc{Dsl}s)
must answer a harder question than syntactic coverage: do computed series and
backtest outcomes remain faithful under floating-point arithmetic, dual execution
hosts, and order-fill semantics?
We present a validation framework for \pyne{}, an independent open-source toolchain
for the \pine{} language, covering numerical indicator accuracy, interpret$\leftrightarrow$compile
plot parity, honest \code{request.security} / $\na$ policy, strategy broker semantics
(entries, exits, commission, slippage, pyramiding, pending fills), alerts, and
drawing resource limits.
Across $85+$ technical analysis indicators and $181$ validated builtins, measured
relative errors are on the order of $10^{-5}$ percent class claims (maximum reported
$0.0022\%$ on ADX under extreme volatility; mean $<0.0005\%$; zero systematic bias;
exact agreement for deterministic SMA/sum families) under IEEE~754 double
arithmetic~\citep{ieee754,higham2002accuracy}.
A dual-host harness compares \code{Runtime.run(..., mode="interpret")} against
\code{mode="compile"} with nan-aware \code{allclose}, bucketizing residuals into
\code{OK}, structural fill/hline-only differences, matched dual errors, and true
value \code{MISMATCH}.
Shared formula alignment for RSI (Wilder), ROC, WMA, cumulative sum, and
highest/lowest bar indices reduces host drift to float noise
($\sim\!10^{-11}$ absolute on aligned kernels).
We formalize a security NA honesty policy (same-symbol simple \ohlcv{} may
passthrough; foreign/complex requests resolve to $\na$ rather than inventing
series), sketch the order lifecycle including F2 pending-fill VWAP under
pyramiding~$\le 0$, and report strategy golden cases for OCA, risk enforcement,
and closed-trade accounting.
We do \emph{not} claim TradingView platform certification; residual mismatch
tails and nested-EMA seed families remain compatibility work items.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Keywords.}
numerical validation; floating-point parity; trading strategy backtesting;
order fill semantics; dual-runtime consistency; technical indicators.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%==============================================================================

Financial \textsc{Dsl}s occupy a peculiar niche in software engineering:
they are simultaneously \emph{specification languages} for mathematical series
and \emph{execution engines} for trading logic whose outputs move capital.
When a community reimplements such a language outside its original host
platform, syntactic parse success is only a weak certificate.
Practitioners care whether $\ta{}.\code{rsi}(\code{close},14)$ on a given
\ohlcv{} history matches reference values within floating-point tolerance,
whether two backends of the same open-source stack agree with each other,
and whether \code{strategy.entry}/\code{strategy.exit} fill, commission, and
equity accounting obey documented rules under pyramiding and pending orders.

\pyne{} (package \code{hoox-pyne}, import \code{pynescript}) is an independent
open-source toolchain for \pine{} v5/v6~\citep{pynescript2026,tradingview-pine}.
It provides a parser/AST stack, an interpreter evaluator, a Numba-backed
compile path~\citep{lam2015numba,lattner2004llvm}, strategy broker logic,
alerts, and drawing primitives.
This paper focuses on \emph{validation and semantics}, not full language
design: we treat numerical fidelity, dual-host consistency, NA/security policy,
and strategy execution as first-class research objects.

\paragraph{Why independent runtime validation matters.}
Reimplementations of market \textsc{Dsl}s face incentives that pure compilers
do not.
(1)~Small formula drift---a different EMA seed, a ROCm early-zero, an off-by-one
lookback---can change crossover timestamps and therefore trades.
(2)~Floating-point non-associativity means mathematically equivalent reductions
need not bit-agree~\citep{higham2002accuracy,press2007numerical,ieee754}.
(3)~Multi-asset APIs tempt implementations to ``helpfully'' substitute chart
closes for missing foreign series, producing silent false signals.
(4)~Backtesters that disagree on pending-fill averaging or commission models
report incompatible Sharpe ratios for the same script~\citep{tsay2010analysis}.
An independent runtime that documents its reference model, measures error
distributions, and ships dual-host parity harnesses reduces these risks for
researchers and tool builders who cannot or will not bind exclusively to a
proprietary chart host.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A threat model for numerical and semantic divergence in finance
    \textsc{Dsl} reimplementations (\S\ref{sec:threat}).
  \item An indicator reference model and Nov~2025 (+2026 dual-host) numerical
    validation campaign covering $85+$ TA indicators and $181$ functions

    (\S\ref{sec:alerts}).
  \item Experimental tables, error histograms, and golden-case summary
    (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Threat Model for Numerical Divergence}
\label{sec:threat}
%==============================================================================

We model an adversary---or more often, an accidental process---that produces
outputs diverging from a stated reference without crashing.
Threats relevant to \pyne{} fall into four families.

\subsection{Formula drift}
\label{sec:threat-formula}
Two implementations may compute ``RSI'' under different classical definitions:
Wilder smoothing versus SMA of gains~\citep{wilder1978new,murphy1999technical},
or different conventions for the first warm-up bar.
Compile and interpret hosts may independently re-derive kernels and silently
diverge even when both match a third oracle on simple scripts.
\emph{Mitigation:} share formula implementations across hosts for hot kernels;
encode goldens; dual-run the same script+\ohlcv{}.

\subsection{Seed and warm-up differences}
Exponential and nested-EMA families (EMA, DEMA, TEMA, MACD signal, Supertrend
inner averages) depend on initial seed choice and the number of bars before
non-$\na$ output.
Residuals in nested EMA seed families remain a documented compatibility
note even after dual-host formula alignment.

\subsection{NA and multi-asset handling}
Pine's $\na$ is both a missing-data token and a control-flow signal.
Divergent policies---propagating $\na$, zero-filling, or substituting chart
\code{close} for foreign \code{request.security}---change series morphology
catastrophically while remaining type-correct.
\emph{Mitigation:} honest NA policy (\S\ref{sec:na}): never invent foreign
closes when no feed is available.

\subsection{Floating-point non-associativity}
IEEE~754 binary64 addition is not associative~\citep{ieee754}.
Windowed sums, online variance, and fused multiply-add reorderings can yield
$O(nu\varepsilon)$ relative differences~\citep{higham2002accuracy}.
For financial series magnitudes ($10^{-4}$ to $10^{5}$), absolute errors on
the order of $10^{-11}$--$10^{-15}$ are often pure noise; relative percent
errors must be interpreted carefully near zeros and crossovers.
\emph{Mitigation:} nan-aware \code{allclose} with dual absolute/relative
tolerances; report max and mean absolute error for kernel goldens.

\section{Indicator Semantics and Reference Model}
\label{sec:indicators}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Series model}
A bar stream is a sequence of records
\begin{equation}
  B_i = (o_i,h_i,l_i,c_i,v_i,t_i),\qquad i=0,\ldots,N-1,
\end{equation}
with open/high/low/close/volume/time.
Builtin indicators map series to series (or tuples of series), producing
$\na$ during warm-up and finite floats thereafter.
We write $x[\baridx]$ for the value of series $x$ at bar $\baridx$.

\subsection{Wilder RSI}
\label{sec:rsi}
Relative Strength Index after Wilder~\citep{wilder1978new} with period
$\period$ uses average gain $G$ and average loss $L$:
\begin{align}
  \Delta_i &= c_i - c_{i-1}, \\
  g_i &= \max(\Delta_i,0),\qquad
  \ell_i = \max(-\Delta_i,0), \\
  G_i &=
  \begin{cases}
    \frac{1}{\period}\sum_{k=0}^{\period-1} g_{i-k} & \text{first seed},\\
    \frac{(\period-1)G_{i-1}+g_i}{\period} & \text{thereafter},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:wilder-g}\\
  L_i &\text{ analogous with }\ell_i, \\
  \mathrm{RS}_i &= G_i / L_i \quad (L_i>0),\qquad
  \mathrm{RSI}_i = 100 - \frac{100}{1+\mathrm{RS}_i}.
  \label{eq:rsi}
\end{align}
\pyne{} dual-host alignment uses this Wilder recurrence on both interpret and
compile paths (2026 shared-formula work), avoiding host-specific early SMA
variants that previously produced plot drift.

\subsection{Deterministic families}
Simple moving averages, cumulative sums, counts, and pure ranking operations
admit exact equality under identical input ordering when no intermediate
rounding differs:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SMA}_i(\period)
  = \frac{1}{\period}\sum_{k=0}^{\period-1} x_{i-k}
  \quad (i\ge \period-1).
\end{equation}
Validation reports $100\%$ exact agreement for SMA/sum/count-style
deterministic ops~\citep{pynescript2026}.

\subsection{Bollinger and envelope constructions}
Bollinger Bands~\citep{bollinger2001} combine SMA with sample or population
standard deviation scaled by a factor $k$; small stdev formula differences
dominate residual error, not the band arithmetic itself.
Trend systems (ADX, DMI, Supertrend) compose multiple smoothers and are the
usual worst-case relative-error loci under high volatility~\citep{murphy1999technical}.

\section{Validation Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Campaign scope}
The November~2025 numerical validation campaign (with 2026 dual-host formula
updates noted separately) covered:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $85+$ technical analysis indicators;
  \item $181$ total builtin functions (math, array, TA, utility);
  \item synthetic and real-market \ohlcv{} regimes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data regimes}
\paragraph{Synthetic (primary).}
$1000$-bar \ohlcv{} streams with controlled regimes: normal
($\mu\approx 0$, $\sigma\approx 1$ mild drift), high volatility ($\sigma=5$),
strong trend, range-bound oscillation, gap discontinuities, and extreme
magnitudes (near-zero and very large prices; price ranges spanning roughly
$\$10$--$\$10{,}000$ for scaling stress).

\paragraph{Real markets (secondary validation).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item SPX: multi-year daily;
  \item BTC/USD: multi-year hourly;
  \item EURUSD: multi-year $15$-minute;
  \item AAPL: multi-year daily.
\end{itemize}
Financial time series structure follows standard market microstructure and
returns analysis assumptions~\citep{tsay2010analysis,box1970time}.

\subsection{Error metrics}
For reference series $r_i$ and implementation series $y_i$ over finite,
paired non-$\na$ indices $I$:
\begin{align}
  e^{\mathrm{abs}}_i &= \lvert r_i - y_i\rvert,
  \label{eq:abs}\\
  e^{\mathrm{rel}}_i &= \frac{\lvert r_i - y_i\rvert}{\lvert r_i\rvert}\cdot 100\%
    \quad (r_i\neq 0),
  \label{eq:rel}\\
  \maxerr &= \max_{i\in I} e^{\mathrm{rel}}_i,
  \label{eq:max}\\
  \meanerr &= \frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{i\in I} e^{\mathrm{rel}}_i.
  \label{eq:mean}
\end{align}
Dual-host kernel goldens additionally report maximum absolute error
$\max_i\lvert r_i-y_i\rvert$ in float units (typically $\sim 10^{-11}$ after
alignment).

\subsection{Acceptance thresholds}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Level & Relative error & Disposition \\
\midrule
Excellent & $<0.001\%$ & Pass \\
Good & $<0.01\%$ & Pass \\
Acceptable & $<0.1\%$ & Review \\
Unacceptable & $\ge 0.1\%$ & Fail \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Bias tests}
Beyond max/mean error, the campaign checks systematic bias:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\delta} = \frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{i\in I}(y_i-r_i).
\end{equation}
Zero systematic bias was reported across the validated set (no consistent
over-/under-estimation pattern after aggregation).

\subsection{Algorithm: single-indicator validation}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ValidateIndicator}
\label{alg:validate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Indicator $f$, bar set $\mathcal{B}$, reference oracle $R$, thresholds
\State $r \gets R(f,\mathcal{B})$;\; $y \gets \mathrm{Pyne}(f,\mathcal{B})$
\State Align indices; drop paired $\na$ warm-up
\State Compute $\abserr,\relerr,\maxerr,\meanerr,\bar{\delta}$
\If{$\maxerr$ exceeds Acceptable}
  \State \Return \textsc{Fail} with diagnostics
\EndIf
\State Record histogram bins; update category tables
\State \Return \textsc{Pass}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Dual-Host Parity Framework}
\label{sec:dual}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Motivation}
Even perfect agreement with an external oracle on one host does not guarantee
that \pyne{}'s interpreter and compiler agree with \emph{each other}.
Users may choose \code{mode="interpret"} for debuggability and
\code{mode="compile"} for speed; silent plot divergence is a product defect.
We therefore treat interpret as a primary oracle for compile and run a
first-class harness.

\subsection{Harness design}
Script \file{scripts/compare\_interp\_compile.py} selects fixtures (default
glob under \file{tests/fixtures/parity/pine/}), builds shared synthetic bars,
and runs:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=python,basicstyle=\ttfamily\scriptsize]
Runtime.run(source, bars, mode="interpret")
Runtime.run(source, bars, mode="compile")
\end{lstlisting}
Plot series in \code{result["series"]} are compared with nan-aware
\code{allclose} defaults $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-5}$, $\mathrm{atol}=10^{-6}$.
Reports write \file{.cache/interp\_compile\_parity.json}.
Always-on tests live in \file{tests/test\_interp\_compile\_parity.py};
foreign-security honesty is covered by
\file{tests/test\_dividend\_yield\_parity.py} and request data-feed tests.

\subsection{Residual taxonomy (buckets)}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lp{8.2cm}}
\toprule
Bucket & Meaning \\
\midrule
\code{OK} & Common series keys allclose; no fatal structural issues \\
\code{fill\_background\_only} & Value OK after ignoring fill/background keys \\
\code{both\_error\_same} & Both hosts raise equivalent normalized errors \\
\code{expected\_error} & Intentional dual-backend demos (e.g.\ pivot depth) \\
\code{both\_error} & Both fail but messages differ after normalize \\
\code{MISMATCH} & Value or type/na disagreement on a shared key \\
\code{interp\_error} / \code{compile\_error} & Single-host failure \\
\code{structural\_only} & Key-set differences without value compare \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Structural one-sided \code{hline} / fill keys can be ignored via harness flags
(\code{--ignore-hline-keys}, \code{--ignore-fill-keys});
\code{--strict-keys} fails on leftover only-interpret/only-compile keys.

\subsection{Shared formula alignment (2026)}
Interpret and compile now share formulas for several hot kernels so dual-run
plots match rather than drift by host-specific quirks:
\code{ta.rsi} (Wilder), \code{ta.roc} (no early zero), \code{ta.wma}
(full non-$\na$ window), \code{ta.cum}, \code{ta.highestbars}/\code{lowestbars}.
Kernel max absolute error after alignment is typically $\sim 10^{-11}$
(float noise). Residual notes (e.g.\ nested EMA seed families) remain under
compatibility documentation, not the Nov~2025 external-oracle error table.

\subsection{Algorithm: dual-host compare}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{CompareInterpCompile}
\label{alg:compare}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Script $S$, bars $\mathcal{B}$, tolerances $(\rho,\alpha)$
\State $I \gets \mathrm{Run}(S,\mathcal{B},\textsc{Interpret})$
\State $C \gets \mathrm{Run}(S,\mathcal{B},\textsc{Compile})$
\If{both raised}
  \State \Return \code{expected\_error} or \code{both\_error\_same}/\code{both\_error}
\ElsIf{exactly one raised}
  \State \Return \code{interp\_error} or \code{compile\_error}
\EndIf
\State Filter series keys (optional hline/fill ignore)
\For{each common key $k$}
  \If{not $\mathrm{NanAllclose}(I_k,C_k,\rho,\alpha)$}
    \State Record $\mathrm{n\_bad}$, $\max\lvert I-C\rvert$, sample indices
    \State \Return \code{MISMATCH}
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{key sets differ and strict}
  \State \Return \code{structural\_only}
\EndIf
\State \Return \code{OK} (or \code{fill\_background\_only})
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{NA and \texttt{request.security} Honesty Policy}
\label{sec:na}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Design principle}
When multi-asset or multi-timeframe data are unavailable, \pyne{} prefers
\emph{honest} $\na$ over inventing foreign closes from the chart series.
Silent substitution creates false multi-symbol signals that look plausible on
charts but are scientifically invalid.

\subsection{Formal rules}
Let $\sigma_{\mathrm{chart}}$ be the chart symbol, $\sigma$ the requested
symbol, $\tau$ the requested timeframe, and $e$ the requested expression.

\begin{definition}[Same-symbol simple OHLCV]
\label{def:same-sym}
A request is \emph{same-symbol simple} if $\sigma=\sigma_{\mathrm{chart}}$
(or an equivalent ticker id) and $e$ is a simple OHLCV projection
(\code{open}/\code{high}/\code{low}/\code{close}/\code{volume} or trivial
aliases) at a timeframe the host can satisfy from the bound bar feed.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Foreign or complex request]
\label{def:foreign}
A request is \emph{foreign/complex} if $\sigma\neq\sigma_{\mathrm{chart}}$,
or $e$ requires multi-symbol inputs, unavailable HTF structure without a
feed, or other host-unavailable data.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Honesty policy]
\label{prop:honest}
Under the default host without an external multi-asset feed:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Same-symbol simple OHLCV may \emph{passthrough} (or lower/raise with
    documented MTF rules) using chart bars.
  \item Foreign tickers and complex \code{request.security} expressions resolve
    to $\na$ (scalar or series of $\na$) on \emph{both} interpret and compile.
  \item Implementations must not substitute $\code{close}_{\mathrm{chart}}$
    for foreign $\code{close}_{\sigma}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Both backends implement the same policy so dual-host parity holds even when
the economically meaningful value is ``missing.''
Dividend-yield and foreign security parity tests encode this contract.

\section{Strategy Execution Model}
\label{sec:strategy}
%==============================================================================

Strategy scripts drive a broker model that maintains positions, pending
orders, cash/equity, commissions, and trade logs
(\code{strategy.closedtrades}, open-trade queries, event streams).
Interpret builtins and the compile \code{strategy\_broker} are kept aligned
through shared goldens (\file{tests/test\_order\_fills.py},
\file{tests/test\_oca\_commission.py},
\file{tests/test\_strategy\_risk\_enforcement.py},
\file{tests/test\_compiler\_strategy.py},
\file{tests/test\_strategy\_events.py}).

\subsection{Order lifecycle}
Orders progress through a finite set of states (Figure~\ref{fig:order-sm}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Submit:} \code{strategy.entry}, \code{strategy.order},
    \code{strategy.exit}, \code{strategy.close}, \ldots
  \item \textbf{Pending:} resting limit/stop or market deferred to bar
    processing;
  \item \textbf{Fill:} price/qty determined from bar OHLC, slippage, and
    sizing rules;
  \item \textbf{Position update:} open trades, VWAP, events;
  \item \textbf{Exit/close:} realize PnL into closed trades; update equity.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[t]

\subsection{Fill price with slippage}
Let $p^{\star}$ be the raw trigger/fill price from OHLC logic and $d\in\{+1,-1\}$
the signed side (buy positive). With \code{slippage} in ticks and mintick
$\mu$:
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{fill}} = p^{\star} + d\cdot s\cdot\mu,
  \label{eq:slip}
\end{equation}
where $s$ is the integer slippage tick count (zero disables).
Market and stop/limit paths apply \eqref{eq:slip} consistently on interpret
and compile brokers.

\subsection{Commission models}
Supported commission kinds (string aliases include
\code{strategy.commission.*} forms):
\begin{align}
  C_{\mathrm{order}} &= c,
  \label{eq:comm-order}\\
  C_{\mathrm{contract}} &= c\cdot q,
  \label{eq:comm-contract}\\
  C_{\mathrm{percent}} &= c\cdot \frac{p_{\mathrm{fill}}\cdot q}{100},
  \label{eq:comm-pct}
\end{align}
with commission rate $c$, quantity $q$, and fill price $p_{\mathrm{fill}}$.
Net trade PnL subtracts entry and exit commissions as appropriate.

\subsection{Default quantity}
Default size resolution includes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{fixed}: constant contracts/units;
  \item \code{cash}: $q = Q_{\mathrm{cash}} / p_{\mathrm{fill}}$;
  \item \code{percent\_of\_equity}:
    $q = E\cdot (\pi/100) / p_{\mathrm{fill}}$ for equity $E$ and percent $\pi$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pyramiding and pending fills (F2 Round~7)}
\label{sec:f2}
Pyramiding bounds how many concurrent entries may stack.
\pyne{} models \code{pyramiding} as maximum \emph{additional} entries
($\mathrm{max\_open}=\mathrm{pyramiding}+1$); this is a deliberate modeling
choice relative to some host document defaults and is recorded as a known
semantic note.

\paragraph{Market entries.}
With $\mathrm{pyramiding}\le 0$, a second distinct entry id is blocked; same-id
replace semantics apply on the market path.

\paragraph{Pending fills (F2).}
When $\mathrm{pyramiding}\le 0$, stacked pending fills in the same direction
\emph{merge} into a single open trade with volume-weighted average price:
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{avg}}'
  = \frac{p_{\mathrm{avg}}\cdot q + p_{\mathrm{fill}}\cdot q_f}{q+q_f},
  \label{eq:vwap}
\end{equation}
retaining the first leg's entry id/bar/time, summing commissions, and
emitting one entry event per fill.
When $\mathrm{pyramiding}>0$, legs may append up to the cap; further fills are
ignored.
Compile broker locks \code{open\_entry\_count}=1 under $\mathrm{pyramiding}\le 0$
for parity with interpret's single-leg book.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ProcessPendingFills (pyramiding $\le 0$ merge path)}
\label{alg:pending}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Pending queue $Q$, bar OHLC, position state $\Pi$, pyramiding $p$
\For{order $o\in Q$ triggered by OHLC}
  \State $p_f,q_f \gets$ fill price/qty with slippage \& commission
  \If{$p\le 0$ and same-direction open exists}
    \State Merge: $q\gets q+q_f$;\; $p_{\mathrm{avg}}\gets$ VWAP \eqref{eq:vwap}
    \State Accumulate commission; keep first entry metadata
  \ElsIf{$p>0$ and open legs $< p+1$}
    \State Append new open leg
  \Else
    \State Ignore fill / enforce cap
  \EndIf
  \State Emit entry/exit events; update equity
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Equity and PnL sketch}
Let cash $K$, position quantity $q$ (signed), average entry $p_{\mathrm{avg}}$,
and mark price $m$. Roughly:
\begin{align}
  E &\approx K + q\cdot(m-p_{\mathrm{avg}}) - C_{\mathrm{open}},
  \label{eq:equity}\\
  \mathrm{PnL}_{\mathrm{closed}}
  &= q_c\cdot(p_{\mathrm{exit}}-p_{\mathrm{entry}}) - C_{\mathrm{entry}}
    - C_{\mathrm{exit}}
  \label{eq:pnl}
\end{align}
(with sign conventions for shorts). Closed trades populate
\code{strategy.closedtrades} accessors used by scripts and tests.
Exact free-cash definitions follow broker helpers (\code{cash} as equity minus
capital locked in open exposure).

\subsection{OCA and risk (high level)}
One-Cancels-All groups cancel sibling orders on fill.
Risk enforcement tests cover max drawdown / position limits style guards at
the strategy surface (see \file{tests/test\_strategy\_risk\_enforcement.py}).
Full broker feature parity with every proprietary host flag
(\code{process\_orders\_on\_close}, \code{calc\_on\_every\_tick}, full margin)
is out of scope; residual gaps are listed in Round~7 F2 notes.

\subsection{Events}
Strategy event streams record entries, exits, and related notifications for
export and debugging; event-order goldens stabilize dual-host and regression
behavior under pyramiding merges.

\section{Alerts and Drawing Resource Limits}
\label{sec:alerts}

\subsection{Alerts}
\code{alert()} and \code{alertcondition()} record structured alert objects
with message, frequency, and context (e.g.\ bar index).
Supported frequencies include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{once\_per\_bar} --- de-duplicate within the bar;
  \item \code{once\_per\_bar\_close} --- fire on confirmed close semantics;
  \item \code{all} --- fire on every trigger evaluation.
\end{itemize}
Tests in \file{tests/test\_alerts.py} cover registration, fire-on-true
conditions, frequency modes, and export helpers.

\subsection{Drawing garbage collection}
Chart drawings are resource-capped to bound memory and match Pine declaration
arguments:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{max\_lines\_count}
  \item \code{max\_labels\_count}
  \item \code{max\_boxes\_count}
  \item \code{max\_polylines\_count}
\end{itemize}
When counts exceed maxima, older primitives are reclaimed (GC), as exercised by
\file{tests/test\_drawing\_gc.py}.
These limits are part of observable script semantics: overflowing drawings
disappear in order, which can affect visual-only strategies but not numeric
series.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%==============================================================================

\paragraph{Numerical reproducibility.}
Higham's treatment of accuracy and stability~\citep{higham2002accuracy} and
Numerical Recipes~\citep{press2007numerical} frame floating-point error as
structural rather than incidental.
IEEE~754~\citep{ieee754} standardizes formats and rounding modes underlying
all \pyne{} numeric paths (Python floats / NumPy / Numba LLVM)~\citep{harris2020numpy,lam2015numba}.
Our contribution is domain-specific: applying these classical tools to a
trading \textsc{Dsl} dual runtime with published percent-error tables.

\paragraph{Technical analysis computation.}
Wilder's systems~\citep{wilder1978new}, Murphy's practitioner synthesis~\citep{murphy1999technical},
and Bollinger's bands~\citep{bollinger2001} define much of the indicator surface.
\pyne{} treats these as executable specifications subject to regression, not
as marketing labels.

\paragraph{Financial time series.}
Tsay~\citep{tsay2010analysis} and Box--Jenkins~\citep{box1970time} provide
statistical context for multi-regime market data used in validation.

\paragraph{Backtester design.}
Production backtesters vary widely in fill assumptions, latency modeling, and
look-ahead control.
We do not claim a novel market-simulator microstructure; we claim
\emph{documented, tested} order semantics (slippage ticks, commission kinds,
pyramiding, pending VWAP) aligned across two hosts inside one open toolchain.

\paragraph{Compiler correctness.}
Classical compiler texts emphasize semantic preservation~\citep{aho2006dragon,appeli1997modern}.
Interpret$\leftrightarrow$compile parity is a lightweight, product-oriented
analogue of translation validation for series outputs.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
%==============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Not TradingView certification.}
    Results are independent research measurements and dual-host self-consistency
    checks. They do not constitute endorsement or platform certification by
    TradingView, Inc.
  \item \textbf{Residual \code{MISMATCH} tail.}
    Some scripts (e.g.\ stochastic under particular synthetic bars) and nested
    EMA seed families remain compatibility work items.
  \item \textbf{Security data plane.}
    Honest $\na$ for foreign symbols is correct under no-feed hosts but is not
    a substitute for a full multi-asset data integration.
  \item \textbf{Strategy model gaps.}
    Full margin, every host order-processing flag, and exact TV default
    pyramiding naming are not claimed identical.
  \item \textbf{Oracle methodology.}
    Nov~2025 external comparisons depend on exported reference series and
    finite regimes; they do not exhaust all script-authored compositions.
  \item \textbf{Performance vs.\ correctness.}
    This paper does not evaluate wall-clock speed; compile exists for
    performance~\citep{lam2015numba} but correctness is the present focus.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
%==============================================================================

Independent finance \textsc{Dsl} runtimes must prove more than parse coverage.
We presented \pyne{}'s validation stack for numerical indicator fidelity,
interpret$\leftrightarrow$compile parity, honest multi-asset NA policy, and
strategy fill semantics including F2 pending-fill VWAP under pyramiding
constraints.
Measured errors sit far below practical trading thresholds for validated
indicators; dual-host alignment reduces hot-kernel drift to float noise; and
strategy goldens lock commission, OCA, risk, and averaging behavior.
Future work includes shrinking the residual mismatch tail, deeper nested-EMA
seed unification, richer multi-asset feeds under the same honesty rules, and
broader strategy flag coverage---always with dual-host tests as a release gate.
